Making stock every week sounds cozy until you realize you are committing to chopping aromatics and babysitting a pot every Sunday. I would rather treat stock day like a proper production: pile on roasted bones, double down on onions and leeks, and simmer slowly until the kitchen smells like a French bistro.

When the stock hits that glossy, gelled stage, I push it even further into demi-glace territory. Reducing by half concentrates the gelatin, and it means I only need a cube or two to turn a quick pan sauce or soup into something layered. Keep a silicone ice tray purely for stock and label the bags once the cubes are frozen solid.

The freezer becomes a palette. Thin cubes for risottos, thick blocks for braises, a few scoops blended with miso for noodle nights. The up-front effort saves me from mediocre broth emergencies and lets weeknight meals taste like they simmered all day.
