Pellet quality is more obvious in the smoke than on the packaging. A fresh bag should smell like the wood it claims to be—sweet and resinous for apple, mellow campfire for oak, almost floral for cherry. If you open the bag and catch more dusty cardboard than wood, it was probably over-dried or blended with filler.

I pinch a few pellets to check density. Tight, glassy pellets hold together even when I squeeze hard, while crumbly pellets break down into sawdust that smolders more than it burns. Consistency matters for the auger too: long pellets jam, ultra-short pellets dump fuel and spike temperatures.

Another giveaway is the binder. The best pellets rely on natural lignin to hold shape, leaving very little oily residue on your hands. If the pellets look waxy or leave a film behind, there is usually a glue or oil binder involved, and that flavor shows up in delicate foods.

Ash tells the final story. I want a light, chalky ash that brushes out quickly. Dark, clumpy ash is a sign of additives or bark-heavy blends, and it smothers airflow between long cooks.

### Woods I reach for

- **Poultry & seafood:** Apple for the aromatic sweetness, pecan when I want more nuttiness, and cherry for rosy color on skin-on pieces.
- **Pork & ribs:** Blend oak and cherry for rich smoke that still lets brown sugar rubs shine.
- **Beef:** Straight hickory or mesquite in small doses to get that steakhouse crust without blowing out the interior.

### Go-to brands

- **Oak-forward:** Lumber Jack Competition Blend or CookinPellets Perfect Mix.
- **Fruit dominant:** Bear Mountain Apple or B&B Cherry.
- **Big smoke energy:** B&B Mesquite or Knotty Wood Plum, both clean even at low temps.
