Counter space is the most valuable real estate in the kitchen, so I'm stingy
with it. Everything else has a cabinet, a drawer, or a shelf. What stays out is
either something I reach for constantly or something that makes the next step
easier. Here's the tour, left to right.

---

## 1. Microwave / Convection Oven

The countertop combo unit holds down the far-left end. It's a microwave when I
need one and a second oven when I need that instead, and it does both without
taking up more than one spot.

## 2. The Flour Bin

Next is a big restaurant-style bin dedicated to **pizza dough flour**. Pizza
comes up a lot around here (we take
[emergency pizza](/travel/msc-yacht-club-2025) seriously), and a bin with a lid
beats wrestling a half-folded bag every time I make dough.

## 3. The Hand-Wash Landing Zone

This spot is **empty on purpose**. It's where anything that needs hand washing
lands: knives, scales, and anything else I don't want banging around in the
sink. Knives dull (and get dangerous) rattling against pots, and scales don't
belong in standing water.

The rule is simple: if this spot is covered, it isn't doing its job.

## 4. The Sink Station

The sink keeps a small, deliberate kit:

- **Green sponge:** the hard one, for scrubbing.
- **Pink sponge:** the soft one, for anything that scratches.
- **Scouring steel:** for the jobs neither sponge can handle.
- **A few soaps and other scrubbers.**

Two colors means nobody has to guess which sponge is safe to use on what.

## 5. Dish Drainer

Right after the sink. Together, the landing zone, sink, and drainer make a
one-way line: dirty on the left, clean on the right.

---

## 6. The Corner: Scales and Mixer Accessories

The corner holds my **scales** and the **KitchenAid accessories** I use most:
the dough hook and the scraping-spatula beater. I weigh nearly everything in
grams, so the scales live out where I can grab them without opening a cabinet.

## 7. KitchenAid Mixer

The mixer sits right next to its accessories. It kneads the pizza dough, and
with attachments it rolls the pasta sheets for the
[Thanksgiving Lasagna](/recipes/thanksgiving-lasagna) and runs the grinder for
[home-ground beef](/kitchen-notes/why-home-ground-beef-is-just-better).

## 8. The Stove (Flanked by Spoon Rests)

There's a spoon rest on each side of the stove, one large and one small, so
whatever I'm stirring has a landing spot no matter which burner it's on.

## 9. The End of the Line

Past the stove, at the very end of the counter, is everything I want within
arm's reach while I'm cooking:

- **Small wine bottles:** single-serve sizes for cooking, like deglazing the
  lasagna sauce, without opening a full bottle.
- **Cooking oils**
- **Dressing oils:** kept separate from the cooking oils.
- **Thermometer**
- **The tools canister** (below)

### The Tools Canister

- **Can opener:** hangs by its handle on the rim.
- **Tongs:** ride the edge, one fork in and one fork out, so they never sink to
  the bottom. They're also how I test the bone on a
  [smoked pork shoulder](/recipes/smoked-pork-shoulder).
- **Spoons, spatulas, and turners.**

---

## Everything Else

Everything else has a cabinet, a drawer, or a shelf. Read left to right, the
counter is really a set of zones: heat and dry storage, then the wash line, then
prep, and finally the stove with everything it needs at arm's reach. If a thing
doesn't belong to one of those zones, or doesn't get used often enough to earn
the space, it goes behind a door.
